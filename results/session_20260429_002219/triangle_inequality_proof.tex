\textbf{Theorem (Triangle Inequality).}  
Let \((V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)\) be an inner‑product space and \(x,y\in V\). Then
\[
\|x+y\|\le \|x\|+\|y\|.
\]

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Step 1 (Degenerate case).}  
If \(x=0\) or \(y=0\) the inequality reduces to \(\|y\|\le\|y\|\) or
\(\|x\|\le\|x\|\), which holds with equality. Hence we may assume
\(x\neq0\) and \(y\neq0\).

\textbf{Step 2 (Square the inequality).}  
Since norms are non‑negative,
\[
\|x+y\|\le\|x\|+\|y\|
\iff
\|x+y\|^{2}\le(\|x\|+\|y\|)^{2}.
\]

\textbf{Step 3 (Expand both sides).}
\begin{align*}
\|x+y\|^{2}
   &=\langle x+y,\,x+y\rangle \\
   &=\langle x,x\rangle+\langle x,y\rangle
     +\langle y,x\rangle+\langle y,y\rangle \\
   &=\|x\|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}\langle x,y\rangle+\|y\|^{2},
\end{align*}
and
\[
(\|x\|+\|y\|)^{2}
   =\|x\|^{2}+2\|x\|\,\|y\|+\|y\|^{2}.
\]

\textbf{Step 4 (Cauchy–Schwarz bound).}  
The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives \(|\langle x,y\rangle|\le\|x\|\,\|y\|\).
Since \(\operatorname{Re}\langle x,y\rangle\le|\langle x,y\rangle|\),
\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle x,y\rangle\le\|x\|\,\|y\|.
\]

\textbf{Step 5 (Insert the bound).}  
Substituting the estimate from Step 4 into the expansion of \(\|x+y\|^{2}\) yields
\begin{align*}
\|x+y\|^{2}
   &=\|x\|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}\langle x,y\rangle+\|y\|^{2}\\
   &\le\|x\|^{2}+2\|x\|\,\|y\|+\|y\|^{2}
    =(\|x\|+\|y\|)^{2}.
\end{align*}

\textbf{Step 6 (Square roots).}  
Both sides of the inequality in Step 5 are non‑negative; taking square roots
preserves the order:
\[
\|x+y\|\le\|x\|+\|y\|.
\]

\textbf{Step 7 (Equality condition).}  
Equality in the triangle inequality occurs precisely when equality holds in
each inequality used above.

\begin{itemize}
\item Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz holds iff there exists \(\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\) such that
\(y=\lambda x\).
\item Equality \(\operatorname{Re}\langle x,y\rangle=|\langle x,y\rangle|\) forces
\(\lambda\) to be a non‑negative real number; otherwise the real part would be
strictly smaller than the modulus.
\end{itemize}
Thus
\[
\|x+y\|=\|x\|+\|y\|
\iff
\bigl(x=0\bigr)\ \text{or}\ \bigl(y=0\bigr)\ \text{or}\ \bigl(y=\lambda x\text{ with }\lambda\ge0\bigr).
\]

In the latter case,
\[
\|x+y\|=\|x+\lambda x\|=(1+\lambda)\|x\|
        =\|x\|+\|\lambda x\|=\|x\|+\|y\|.
\]

Hence the triangle inequality holds for all vectors, and equality holds
iff the vectors are positively linearly dependent (including the zero‑vector
cases). \qed
\end{proof}
